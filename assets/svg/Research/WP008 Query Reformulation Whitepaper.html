ABSTRACT

This paper documents pre-build validation research for a Complex Query Reformulation module planned as a future feature addition to AI Spider, a proprietary locally-hosted website crawler and AI retrievability audit platform. It is the eighth paper in a series validating AI Spider's content intelligence layer, following WP001-007.

WP001-007 all operated on the same assumption: that a query is a fixed starting point and pages should be evaluated against it. Complex Query Reformulation challenges that assumption. It analyses the query itself, decomposing it into five layers - Core Intent, Sub-Intents, Decision Factors, Hidden Questions, and Alternative Queries - before any page analysis begins. Pages are then evaluated against this complete intent model, not against the literal query words.

The module addresses a fundamental gap: a page that covers "AI visibility tools features and pricing" answers the surface query but may miss "does this tool support agency multi-client use?" - a sub-intent that the user never articulated but that shapes their selection decision. Intent Coverage Score measures what percentage of the complete intent model a page addresses.

Research Conclusion

Complex Query Reformulation is a valid and discriminating signal for AI retrievability. All three frontier models produced coherent five-layer intent models and correctly scored both target pages below 80% intent coverage - confirming that Semrush and WebFX both have intent gaps despite their strong performance on earlier modules. The most important finding is the hidden questions layer: Claude and ChatGPT identified risk-and-trust hidden questions for V2 that Gemini missed entirely - questions like "Will I get assigned to junior staff?" and "What happens if the agency underperforms?" These are the questions that actually prevent conversions, and they are present in Claude and ChatGPT's outputs but absent from Gemini's. Claude remains the recommended default for this module, with ChatGPT as a strong alternative.

SECTION 1
Background and Research Motivation

The eight-module AI Spider content intelligence research series has progressively mapped different dimensions of content quality for AI retrievability. WP001-007 measured what pages contain: entity gaps, coverage completeness, topical focus, confidence signals, entity universe, information density, and reasoning depth. Each measured a different structural property of the page against a given query.

"Every module in WP001-007 treated the query as a fixed, fully-understood starting point. But users do not search in perfectly expressed sentences. When someone types 'best digital marketing agency,' they are asking at least ten distinct questions simultaneously - most of which they will never write down. Complex Query Reformulation makes those questions visible before content analysis begins."

This matters because AI systems do not retrieve pages based on literal keyword matching. When a user asks ChatGPT "best AI visibility tools," the model internally reformulates that query into its intent components - it retrieves pages that address what the user is trying to decide, not just what words they used. A page optimised against the literal query may still be passed over in favour of a page that more completely addresses the full intent model.

The module is also the natural companion to WP005 (Query Expansion). Where WP005 generated what topics a complete answer should contain, WP008 generates what decisions a complete answer should support. "Integration capabilities" is a topic (WP005). "I need to evaluate whether this tool integrates with my existing analytics stack before committing" is a decision intent (WP008). Both are needed; they serve different downstream analytical functions.

  • Q1Sub-intent completeness: Do models identify the same hidden sub-intents, or do they miss important decision factors? The hidden questions layer is the most differentiated - it captures what users think but do not type.
  • Q2Query type sensitivity: V1 ("Best AI visibility tools") is an evaluation query; V2 ("Best digital marketing agency") is a selection/hiring query. These are structurally different intent types. Do models produce meaningfully different intent models for each?
  • Q3Intent Coverage accuracy: When applied to target pages, do models score coverage at levels consistent with manual ground truth (45-55% for Semrush, 50-60% for WebFX)? Models that score pages above 75% have defined intents too narrowly - essentially mapping the intent model to what the page already covers.
SECTION 2
Research Methodology
2.1
The Five-Layer Intent Model

The module decomposes every query into five distinct layers. Each layer captures a different dimension of what the user is trying to accomplish. Together they form the Query Intent Universe - the reference set against which page content is evaluated.

Core Intent
The single, clear statement of what the user is ultimately trying to accomplish. One sentence. Used as the anchor for evaluating all downstream layers.
Sub-Intents
The specific goals that together make up the core intent. 6-10 items. These are what users are actively trying to accomplish when they search. They map directly to content sections a page should address.
Decision Factors
The criteria users weigh when choosing between options. 6-8 items. These are the evaluation dimensions - the lenses users apply to compare alternatives. A page that addresses decision factors reduces friction at the point of choice.
Hidden Questions
Questions users have but do not articulate. 4-6 items. These are the concerns that drive hesitation or prevent conversion. They often involve risk, trust, or qualification - "will this work for my specific situation?" AI models that answer hidden questions are far more likely to be cited as comprehensive sources.
Alt. Queries
How the same intent might be expressed differently. 4-6 alternatives. Used by RankScan to expand monitoring scope and by Query Expansion (WP005) as a complementary vocabulary layer.
Figure 1. The five intent layers and their downstream uses. The Hidden Questions layer is the most analytically novel - it captures the unarticulated concerns that shape user behaviour. A page that addresses hidden questions is not just more complete; it is more trustworthy to the user and more citable to an AI model constructing a comprehensive answer.
2.2
The Four-Stage Prompt

The module uses a four-stage prompt that is unique in the series: Stage 1 generates the intent model with no page content provided, Stage 2 evaluates the target page against it, Stage 3 estimates competitor coverage, and Stage 4 generates missing-intent recommendations ranked by decision impact.

Prompt Template - v1.0  ·  All 3 models identical Page content in Stages 2-4 only

// SYSTEM ROLE

You are a Complex Query Reformulation engine. Decompose the user PROMPT into its complete intent structure, then evaluate how well TARGET and COMPETITOR pages address that intent.

Stage 1 only: generate from your understanding of user intent. Do NOT use external knowledge in Stages 2-4.

// STAGE 1 - QUERY INTENT MODEL (no page content)

a) Core Intent - one clear statement
b) Sub-Intents - 6-10 specific goals
c) Decision Factors - 6-8 evaluation criteria
d) Hidden Questions - 4-6 unarticulated concerns
e) Alternative Queries - 4-6 alternative formulations
f) Query Intent Universe - flat list with total count

// STAGES 2-4

STAGE 2: Target page coverage - checkmarks/crosses / Intent Coverage Score (X/Y = Z%)
STAGE 3: Competitor intent coverage estimates
STAGE 4: Priority 1/2/3 missing intents ranked by decision impact + competitive gap
No commentary outside the format.
Figure 2. Full prompt template. The four-stage structure mirrors WP005 (Query Expansion) but with a fundamentally different Stage 1 objective: generating decision intent rather than topic topics. The "hidden questions" instruction is the most consequential design decision - it forces models to articulate the concerns users have but do not type, which is the layer most directly predictive of conversion and citation quality.

SECTION 4
Key Findings
F1
Intent Coverage is a valid, discriminating signal
All three models correctly identified that both target pages have meaningful intent coverage gaps - Semrush at 52-75% and WebFX at 52-83%. No model scored either page above 85%, confirming that intent-based evaluation surfaces gaps that topic-based evaluation misses. The module measures what users are trying to decide, not just what topics are present.
F2
Gemini's hidden questions layer is structurally incomplete for selection queries
Gemini identified only 1 of 4 key trust/risk hidden questions for V2: it missed "will I get senior or junior staff?", "how do they measure success?", and "are they AI-search ready?" - all of which are primary conversion blockers for agency selection. This is Gemini's most consequential failure in the series because these are the exact questions AI models surface when answering "best digital marketing agency."
F3
Claude identified an AI-readiness hidden question that neither competitor found
Claude's V2 hidden questions included "Are they keeping up with AI search and emerging channels?" - a 2026-specific concern that ChatGPT and Gemini did not generate. This is the question that differentiates a forward-looking agency evaluation from a generic one, and it is the question that makes WebFX's AI search and RevenueCloudFX positioning directly relevant to the user's intent.
F4
ChatGPT over-estimates Intent Coverage consistently
ChatGPT scored Semrush at 75% (GT: ~45-55%) and WebFX at 83% (GT: ~50-60%). This is the same over-inclusion pattern documented across WP002-007. ChatGPT's intent models are comprehensive but its coverage evaluation is permissive - it classifies partial or implied coverage as full coverage, inflating scores and reducing the module's ability to surface actionable gaps.
F5
The three highest-priority missing intents are consistent across all models - and actionable
For V1 Semrush, all three models independently identified the same top-3 missing intents: free trial absence, integration capabilities (MCP/API/BI tools), and LLM platform coverage breadth. For V2 WebFX, all three identified: pricing transparency, company size/industry fit, and measurement/reporting approach. These six intents map directly to specific editorial additions - they are not generic advice but specific page sections or signals that need to be added. Claude's Priority 1 for WebFX: "Add transparent pricing information or at minimum an engagement model description - pricing transparency is the single most common hidden question in agency selection and is absent from WebFX entirely." This is the module's highest-value output.
F6
WP008 surfaces a new category of gap that WP001-007 could not detect
The WebFX page scored HIGH on WP006 (Retrieval Compression), correctly HIGH on WP007 (Multi-Hop Reasoning), and strong on WP004 (Coverage vs Confidence). But it scores 52% on WP008 Intent Coverage because it does not address pricing transparency, company size fit, measurement approach, or timeline expectations - all of which are decision-critical intents for an agency selection query. These are not topic gaps (WP002) or reasoning gaps (WP007); they are decision-process gaps. A prospective client reading the WebFX page cannot answer "is this the right agency for my budget and company size?" - and that unanswered question prevents conversion even when all other content quality dimensions are strong.
Series finding across WP001-008

Across eight consecutive benchmarks, Claude Sonnet 4.6 has been the most calibrated and insight-rich model in every paper. In WP008 it produced the only Intent Coverage scores within the manually established ground truth ranges for both verticals, and it alone identified the AI-readiness hidden question (F3) that makes the intent model genuinely 2026-contextual. ChatGPT GPT-5.5 has been consistently production-ready with a systematic over-inclusion tendency now documented across eight different analytical dimensions. Gemini 3.5 Flash has shown a specific failure pattern in each paper - and WP008 reveals its most practically significant limitation: it produces functionally complete intent models but misses the emotional and trust dimensions of high-stakes selection queries. The AI Spider content intelligence suite uses Claude as the recommended default across all eight modules.

SECTION 5
Implications for AI Spider
  • 01
    Query type classifier runs before intent model generation
    Finding F2 establishes that evaluation queries and selection queries require different intent model depths - particularly in the hidden questions layer. The module will classify the query type (evaluation, selection, educational, navigational) before generating the intent model, and apply type-specific hidden question prompts. Selection queries will receive trust, risk, and relationship hidden question templates; evaluation queries will receive comparison, feature, and accessibility templates.
  • 02
    Hidden Questions panel displayed first in UI
    Finding F5 establishes that the most actionable output is the missing intent identification - particularly the hidden questions that are absent from the page. The module UI will lead with a "Unanswered Questions" panel showing the hidden questions from the intent model that the page does not address. These are the conversion blockers - the concerns that prevent a user from choosing this page as a sufficient answer.
  • 03
    Intent Coverage Score calibration for ChatGPT users
    Finding F4 establishes that ChatGPT consistently over-estimates Intent Coverage (75% vs GT 45-55% for Semrush; 83% vs GT 50-60% for WebFX). If a user selects ChatGPT as their API provider, the module will display a calibration note: "Intent Coverage scores from GPT models tend to run 15-25 percentage points higher than Claude due to more permissive coverage evaluation. Gaps identified may be understated."
  • 04
    Intent Universe shared with WP005 Query Expansion
    WP008's Query Intent Universe and WP005's Query Expansion Universe are complementary. WP005 generates what topics a complete answer should contain; WP008 generates what decisions a complete answer should support. Both are stored in AI Spider's shared universe database for the query. Coverage Score (WP002) uses WP005's topic list; Intent Coverage (WP008) uses its own intent list. Together they provide both topic completeness and decision completeness evaluation.
  • 05
    WP008 closes the series - finding F6 completes the eight-module picture
    Finding F6 establishes that WP008 surfaces a category of gap that WP001-007 cannot detect: decision-process gaps. A page can be topically complete (WP002), focused (WP003), evidence-rich (WP004), entity-complete (WP005), fact-dense (WP006), reasoning-deep (WP007), and still fail to address the specific decisions a user needs to make before converting. The eight-module AI Spider content intelligence suite now covers every dimension of content quality relevant to AI retrievability - and MarAI can generate the specific content types each missing intent calls for.
