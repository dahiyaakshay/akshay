ABSTRACT

This paper documents pre-build validation research for a Competitive Content Intelligence (CCI) module planned as a feature addition to AI Spider, a proprietary website crawler and AI retrievability audit platform. Before committing to building the module, this research was conducted to answer two questions: (1) Is structured content gap analysis a valid signal for predicting and improving AI citation likelihood? (2) Which class of language model performs reliably enough to power this analysis at scale?

The research involved designing a structured 6-dimension analysis prompt, selecting a controlled test set of five competing AI visibility tools, running the prompt across four language models of varying scale and capability, and scoring outputs against a manually derived ground truth. The findings validate the core premise of the CCI module and reveal a clear capability threshold below which smaller local models fail at the multi-source reasoning required.

Research Conclusion

Structured content gap analysis is a valid and actionable signal for AI retrievability improvement. Large frontier models (Claude Sonnet 4.6, ChatGPT GPT-5.5, Gemini 3.5 Flash) perform reliably on this task. Models below the 7B parameter threshold fail at multi-source role tracking and produce unreliable gap attribution. The CCI module will be built using cloud LLM APIs with user-supplied keys.

SECTION 1
Background and Research Motivation

The shift from keyword-based search to AI-generated answers has created a new visibility problem for brands and local businesses. In traditional SEO, ranking was largely a function of authority signals, backlinks, and keyword coverage. In generative search, a page must also satisfy a different criterion: it must contain the information an AI model expects to see in order to cite it as a source.

This creates a gap that existing SEO tools do not adequately address. Most crawlers and audit platforms measure technical health, keyword coverage, and backlink profiles. None of them measure content completeness relative to what AI models actually cite.

"A page can rank on page one for a target keyword and still be completely invisible in AI-generated answers - not because of technical issues, but because it is missing the specific entities, topics, and terminology that competing pages contain."

AI Spider, the platform I am building, performs technical AI retrievability auditing - checking for crawlability, structured data, content signals, and citation readiness. The Competitive Content Intelligence module is a planned addition that would extend AI Spider's capability into the content layer: identifying precisely what information a page is missing relative to its competitive set, and surfacing that as actionable recommendations.

Before building this module, two questions needed validation:

  • Q1Is the premise valid? Does a structured content gap analysis between a target page and its competitors actually surface meaningful, actionable differences that would affect AI citation likelihood?
  • Q2Which model is right for this task? The module will use an LLM to perform the analysis. Do all LLMs perform adequately, or is there a meaningful capability threshold? Can a smaller local model be used to keep costs down and preserve offline functionality?
SECTION 2
Research Methodology
2.1
The CCI Analysis Framework

The Competitive Content Intelligence framework analyses one target URL against a set of competitor URLs across six structured dimensions. Each dimension is designed to surface a specific type of content gap that is known to affect AI retrievability - based on published GEO research and observed citation patterns across ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and Perplexity.

DIM 01
Information Gain
Unique value the target provides that competitors lack
DIM 02
Entity Gap
Named entities competitors mention that the target omits
DIM 03
Topic Gap
Subject areas covered by competitors but absent from the target
DIM 04
Phrase Gap
Industry terminology competitors use that the target page lacks
DIM 05
Coverage Score
Percentage of total competitor topics addressed by the target
DIM 06
Recommendations
Prioritised, specific content additions ranked by likely impact
Figure 1. The six analytical dimensions of the CCI framework. All dimensions are derived purely from the provided page content - no external knowledge is used by the model.
2.2
Test Set Selection

To ensure the benchmark was meaningful, a controlled test set was selected from a single industry vertical: AI visibility and GEO (Generative Engine Optimisation) tools. All five pages address overlapping topics - AI citation tracking, brand visibility, share of voice, competitor benchmarking - but each from a distinct product and company.

  • TTarget: Semrush AI Visibility Toolkit - Product knowledge base page for the Semrush AI Visibility Toolkit. Marketing and feature-focused. Data-rich but technically narrow in GEO vocabulary.
  • C1Peec AI - AI search analytics platform. Strong on metrics framing (visibility, position, sentiment). MCP integration mentioned.
  • C2Rankshift - AI visibility tracker covering ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude, Gemini, Mistral, Llama. Unique for AI crawler log monitoring and UI-based (not API) response capture.
  • C3Promptwatch - GEO platform with content creation features. Notable for Entity Tracker, agentic shopping data, and publish-to-citation timeline.
  • C4OtterlyAI - Content Intelligence Platform. Highest user count (30,000+). Gartner recognition. Microsoft Copilot tracking. Free GEO tools and marketplace ecosystem.

This vertical was chosen deliberately because the researcher has deep domain knowledge of the subject matter, enabling accurate manual ground-truth assessment of model output quality.

2.4
Prompt Design

A single standardised prompt was designed containing the full extracted text of all five pages, a precise task description, and a strict output format specifying all six analytical dimensions with required sub-fields. All models received the identical prompt with no model-specific modifications. Page content was pre-processed to remove navigation, footer, and boilerplate elements, retaining only substantive content signals.

The prompt was designed to be entirely self-contained - explicitly instructing models to use only the provided content and no external knowledge. This was necessary to ensure outputs reflected genuine content gap reasoning rather than model memorisation of the target companies. The full prompt structure is reproduced below with page content omitted for brevity.

Prompt Template v1.0 · All models identical
Page content omitted
// SYSTEM ROLE
You are a Competitive Content Intelligence engine. Your task is to analyze one TARGET page against four COMPETITOR pages and produce a structured content gap report.

Do not use any external knowledge. Base your entire analysis ONLY on the text provided below.
// INPUT STRUCTURE
## TARGET PAGE
**URL:** [target URL]
**Tool:** [tool name]

**Extracted Content:**
[Full extracted page text - navigation, footer, and boilerplate removed]

---

## COMPETITOR 1
**URL:** [competitor URL]
**Tool:** [tool name]

**Extracted Content:**
[Full extracted page text]

... repeated for COMPETITOR 2, 3, 4
// TASK INSTRUCTION
## YOUR TASK

Analyze the TARGET page content against ALL FOUR COMPETITOR pages and produce the following structured report. Use ONLY the content provided above.
// REQUIRED OUTPUT FORMAT (strict - 6 sections)
**1. INFORMATION GAIN ANALYSIS**
List unique information present on the TARGET page that none of the competitors mention.
- Unique sentences / concepts: [list]
- Unique entities: [list]
- Unique phrases: [list]
- Information Gain Score: [0-100]

**2. ENTITY GAP ANALYSIS**
List entities (brands, tools, platforms, companies) mentioned by competitors but ABSENT from the target.
- Missing entities: [list]
- Entities present on target: [list]
- Entity overlap %: [number]

**3. TOPIC GAP ANALYSIS**
List topics covered by competitors but NOT covered by the target page.
- Missing topics: [list]
- Topics covered by target: [list]
- Topic overlap %: [number]

**4. PHRASE GAP ANALYSIS**
List important phrases and terminology used by competitors but absent from the target.
- Missing phrases: [list]
- Unique phrases on target: [list]
- Phrase overlap score: [0-100]

**5. COVERAGE SCORE**
Measure how comprehensively the target covers concepts discussed across all competitor pages.
- Total unique topics across all competitor pages: [number]
- Topics covered by target page: [number]
- Coverage Score: [percentage]
- Missing coverage areas: [list]

**6. RECOMMENDATIONS**
Provide a prioritised list of specific improvements based on the gaps identified above.
- Priority 1 (High Impact): [list]
- Priority 2 (Medium Impact): [list]
- Priority 3 (Lower Impact): [list]

Respond only with the structured report above. Do not add commentary, disclaimers, or explanations outside the report format.
Figure 2. Full prompt template used across all four models. Colour coding: green = role constraint, blue = input blocks, amber = task instruction, purple = output format, red = strict enforcement. Page content replaced with italicised placeholders. The constraint "Do not use any external knowledge" was the single most important design decision - without it, models draw on memorised product information rather than the provided text.
Key Prompt Design Decisions

Self-contained content: All five pages were embedded inline rather than referenced by URL. This eliminated network dependency and ensured every model reasoned from identical input, making outputs directly comparable.

Strict format enforcement: The final instruction ("Respond only with the structured report above") was critical. Without it, smaller models appended commentary and caveats that inflated token usage and broke downstream parsing.

No external knowledge: The explicit prohibition on external knowledge was the most consequential design decision. Without it, models supplemented gaps with their training data - producing confident-sounding but unverifiable additions that could not be attributed to the competitor set.

Six dimensions, not one: Early prototypes used a single "find the gaps" instruction. Splitting into six named dimensions dramatically improved output consistency and reduced hallucination rate across all models tested.

SECTION 4
Key Findings

The benchmark produced four findings with direct implications for the design of the CCI module.

F1
The premise is validated
All three frontier models produced coherent, accurate, actionable content gap reports from the same prompt. The methodology reliably surfaces entity, topic, and phrase gaps that a subject-matter expert independently confirmed as real and relevant to AI citation likelihood.
F2
3B models fail at multi-source reasoning
Qwen2.5 3B exhibited critical role confusion - attributing competitor content to the target page - invalidating its entire output. This is not a prompt engineering failure. It is a fundamental reasoning capacity limitation at 3B scale when tracking 5 distinct content sources simultaneously.
F3
Claude scores best on depth; ChatGPT on breadth
Claude produced fewer but more precise gaps (14 topics vs ChatGPT's 37), with significantly higher recommendation specificity - identifying conversion friction, buyer personas, and vocabulary alignment that ChatGPT missed or listed generically. ChatGPT identified more raw entities but included noise.
F4
The minimum viable model threshold is ~7B+
Based on this benchmark, a locally-hosted model powering the CCI module would need to be at least 7B parameters to handle multi-source role tracking reliably. The module will therefore use cloud APIs with user-supplied keys rather than bundled local inference.

"The most practically significant finding is not which model scored highest - it is that the 3B local model failed in the specific way most dangerous for a production feature: silently producing plausible-looking but inverted output, with no error signal."

SECTION 5
Implications for AI Spider and Agency Use

This research was conducted specifically to inform build decisions for AI Spider's Competitive Content Intelligence module. The findings have direct implications for both module architecture and for the broader application of this methodology in agency client work.

  • 01
    CCI module will use cloud API with user-supplied key
    Local model inference is not viable for this task at any parameter size currently practical for desktop deployment. The module will integrate via OpenAI, Anthropic, or Google API endpoints, with the user providing their own key. This keeps the core AI Spider platform dependency-free while enabling the feature for users who want it.
  • 02
    Analysis will be scoped to URL level, not site level
    URL-level comparison produces better signal quality, lower token cost, and more actionable recommendations than site-level aggregation. This aligns with findings from the benchmark - the models performed best when given focused, well-scoped content rather than broad site summaries.
  • 03
    Entity gap is the highest-value dimension for AI retrievability
    Across the benchmark, entity gaps - missing named tools, platforms, techniques, and organisations - were the most consistently identified and most actionable output. In the AI search context, entities are primary citation triggers. A page that lacks the entities an AI model associates with a topic will not be cited regardless of its authority or technical health.
  • 04
    Vocabulary alignment is an underserved gap in current SEO tooling
    The phrase gap dimension revealed that several pages were technically comprehensive but used outdated or non-standard terminology. The Semrush target page, for example, used "AI search" exclusively throughout, missing the dominant category term "GEO" used by all four competitors. AI models trained on current content are sensitive to this vocabulary mismatch.
  • 05
    The methodology is directly applicable to agency client audits
    The CCI workflow requires no specialised tooling beyond URL access and an LLM API call. It can be applied manually to any client page in any vertical using the standardised prompt template developed in this research. The benchmark confirms that a single well-structured prompt reliably surfaces gaps that would take a human analyst several hours to compile manually.

Next Steps

This paper documents the validation phase. The following work is now planned or in progress:

  • 6.1Module build - AI Spider CCI tab - Implementation of the Competitive Content Intelligence module as a standalone tab within AI Spider. Independent from the core crawl. Accepts target URL plus up to 4 competitor URLs. Outputs all six analytical dimensions in a structured results dashboard.
  • 6.2Multi-vertical prompt testing - The current benchmark used a single vertical (AI/GEO/SEO tools). Additional testing is planned across law, healthcare, insurance, and construction verticals to validate prompt robustness across different content types and entity vocabularies.
  • 6.3Longitudinal citation tracking - Following CCI-informed content updates, AI citation rates will be tracked across ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and Perplexity using RankScan to measure whether closing the identified gaps correlates with measurable citation improvement.
  • 6.47B+ local model re-evaluation - Once Llama 3.1 8B or Qwen2.5 7B are tested on the same prompt, a minimum viable local model recommendation will be published as an addendum to this paper.
About AI Spider

AI Spider is a locally-hosted website crawler and AI retrievability auditing platform modelled on Screaming Frog, built in Node.js/Express with SQLite and a React 19/TypeScript/Vite frontend. It audits pages for AI crawlability, structured data quality, content signals, entity coverage, and citation readiness. The Competitive Content Intelligence module documented in this paper is planned as a future feature addition.
