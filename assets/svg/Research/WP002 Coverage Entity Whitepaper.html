ABSTRACT

This paper documents pre-build validation research for a Coverage Score and Entity Expansion module planned as a future feature addition to AI Spider, a proprietary locally-hosted website crawler and AI retrievability audit platform. The module extends AI Spider's existing deterministic audit capabilities into the content intelligence layer answering not just whether a page is technically crawlable by AI, but whether it contains what AI models expect to see in order to cite it as a source.

Unlike the Competitive Content Intelligence module validated in Working Paper No. 001 (which compares pages against each other), this module introduces a three-stage reasoning workflow: the LLM first generates an independent expected topic set for a given user query, then evaluates the target page against those expectations, then identifies entity gaps relative to competitor pages. This is a harder task it requires the model to reason about completeness from first principles, not just perform pairwise comparison.

Research involved designing a structured three-stage prompt, building a two-vertical test set (AI/GEO tools and roofing/home services), establishing ground truth manually for both verticals, running the identical prompt across four language models, and scoring outputs against that ground truth.

Research Conclusion

Prompt-driven coverage scoring is a valid signal for AI retrievability improvement when powered by a frontier model. Claude Sonnet 4.6 and ChatGPT GPT-5.5 produce calibrated, accurate coverage scores. Gemini 3.5 Flash introduces a specific failure mode unique to this module: under-generating the expected topic set in Stage 1, which mechanically inflates the coverage score. Qwen2.5 3B fails completely for the second consecutive benchmark. The module will be built using cloud LLM APIs with user-supplied keys, defaulting to Claude or GPT-4-class models.

SECTION 1
Background and Research Motivation

Working Paper No. 001 established that structured content gap analysis comparing a target page against competitor pages across six dimensions is a valid signal for AI retrievability improvement, and identified Claude Sonnet 4.6 as the most reliable model for that task. That research validated the Competitive Content Intelligence (CCI) module for AI Spider.

This paper addresses a distinct but related question. CCI tells you what competitors have that you don't. The Coverage Score and Entity Expansion module answers a different question: does your page contain what a complete, authoritative answer to the user's query should contain regardless of what competitors happen to cover?

"A page can match or exceed its competitors in content depth and still be incomplete in absolute terms missing topics that any expert would expect a comprehensive answer to include, and entities that AI models have learned to associate with the query."

This distinction matters because AI models do not only cite pages that outperform competitors. They cite pages that are sufficiently complete relative to the query's information need. A page in a low-quality competitive set could outperform all competitors and still be invisible in AI-generated answers because it fails an absolute completeness threshold.

The Coverage Score and Entity Expansion module was designed to surface this absolute completeness gap. It is planned as a future addition to AI Spider, sitting alongside the existing CCI module and deterministic technical audit capabilities. Before building it, two validation questions needed answering:

  • Q1Can LLMs generate accurate expected topic sets? Stage 1 of the module asks the model to generate what a complete answer should contain independently, from its understanding of the query. Is this generation reliable and calibrated across different verticals?
  • Q2Does topic count in Stage 1 affect coverage accuracy downstream? If models generate different numbers of expected topics, do their coverage scores diverge meaningfully? And which direction does error go over- or under-stating coverage?
  • Q3Does the methodology generalise across domains? WP001 tested only the AI/GEO tools vertical. This research adds a second vertical roofing and home services to validate domain-agnostic reliability.
SECTION 2
Research Methodology
2.1
The Three-Stage Reasoning Framework

The Coverage Score and Entity Expansion module introduces a more demanding analytical structure than the CCI module. Where CCI performed direct pairwise comparison, this module requires the LLM to reason in three sequential stages each dependent on the quality of the previous.

STAGE 01
Expected topic generation
Given the user's query, the model independently generates what a complete, authoritative answer should contain. This is generated from query understanding not from the pages provided. It establishes the completeness ceiling.
Query: "Best AI visibility tools"
Expected: Pricing, platform coverage, citation tracking, entity tracking, GEO features, integrations...
STAGE 02
Coverage evaluation
The target page is evaluated against the Stage 1 expected topic set. Each expected topic is marked covered or missing. Coverage Score = covered topics / total expected topics, expressed as a percentage.
Target covers: Pricing, Sentiment, Share of Voice
Missing: Citation tracking, Entity tracker, GEO, MCP
Score: 12/20 = 60%
STAGE 03
Entity gap analysis
Named entities brands, tools, platforms, concepts, certifications present on competitor pages but absent from the target are identified. Entity Coverage Score = target entities / total competitor entities.
Competitors mention: Perplexity, MCP, Mistral, Llama, Gartner
Target missing: all five
Entity gap flagged for each
Figure 1. The three-stage reasoning framework. Stage 1 quality is the ceiling for all downstream outputs a model that generates too few expected topics in Stage 1 will systematically overstate coverage in Stage 2. This dependency makes Stage 1 the most critical and most differentiating part of the task.

This three-stage structure is significantly more demanding than the CCI prompt from WP001. It requires the model to (1) activate domain knowledge about query completeness, (2) track which topics are and are not addressed in the target content, and (3) perform named entity comparison across multiple sources all in a single prompt and output. The prompt was designed to make each stage explicit and independently scorable.

2.2
Prompt Design

A single standardised prompt was designed for both verticals. The prompt makes each stage explicit with a header and required output format, instructs the model to generate Stage 1 independently from its query understanding (not from the provided pages), and prohibits use of external knowledge in Stages 2 and 3. This distinction use your knowledge for Stage 1, use only provided content for Stages 2 and 3 is the most important structural feature of the prompt.

Prompt Template — v1.0 All 4 models identical
Both verticals · content omitted

// SYSTEM ROLE

You are a Coverage Score and Entity Expansion engine. You will complete THREE stages of reasoning:

STAGE 1 — Generate what a complete, authoritative answer to the user PROMPT should contain.
STAGE 2 — Evaluate how completely the TARGET page covers those expected topics.
STAGE 3 — Identify entity gaps between the TARGET page and the COMPETITOR pages.

Do not use external knowledge in Stages 2 and 3. Base Stages 2 and 3 ONLY on the content provided. Stage 1 should reflect your understanding of what a complete answer to the prompt requires.

// INPUT

## USER PROMPT
[query — e.g. "Best AI visibility tools"]

## TARGET PAGE
URL, tool name, extracted content

## COMPETITOR 1 through 4
[extracted content for each competitor]

// OUTPUT FORMAT — strictly enforced

STAGE 1 — EXPECTED TOPIC SET
- Expected topics: [numbered list]
- Total expected topics: [number]

STAGE 2 — COVERAGE SCORE
- Topics covered by target: [list with checkmarks]
- Topics missing from target: [list with crosses]
- Coverage Score: [covered / total as percentage]

STAGE 3 — ENTITY GAP ANALYSIS
- Entities present on target: [list]
- Entities on competitors but absent from target: [list]
- Entity Coverage Score: [percentage]
- Recommended entity additions: [list]

RECOMMENDATIONS
- Priority 1 / 2 / 3 with specific content additions
Respond only with the structured report. No commentary or explanations outside the format.
Figure 2. Prompt template used across all four models. The key structural distinction from WP001: Stage 1 explicitly permits use of the model's query understanding, while Stages 2 and 3 are restricted to provided content only. This split was the most consequential prompt design decision without it, models blend their training knowledge into coverage evaluation, making Stages 2 and 3 unverifiable.
2.3
Two-Vertical Test Set

Two verticals were selected to test domain generalisability a concern not addressed in WP001. If coverage scoring works reliably only for the AI/GEO tools vertical (a domain models know well from training data), the methodology cannot be deployed as a general-purpose feature.

  • V1AI/GEO Tools "Best AI visibility tools" Target: Semrush AI Visibility Toolkit (KB page). Competitors: Peec AI, Rankshift, Promptwatch, OtterlyAI. Ground truth expected topics: 17. Target coverage baseline: ~35%.
  • V2Roofing and Home Services "How to choose a roofing contractor" Target: This Old House roofing guide. Competitors: Angi (two pages), Bob Vila. Ground truth expected topics: 17. Target coverage baseline: ~24%.

Ground truth for both verticals was established manually before running any model tests. The roofing vertical was selected specifically because it is a practical, high-intent local business topic far from the technical AI/GEO domain making it a meaningful test of whether Stage 1 topic generation degrades when the domain is outside the model's most-practised territory.

SECTION 3
Benchmark Results
3.1
Stage 1 — Expected Topic Generation Quality

Stage 1 quality was the most revealing dimension of this benchmark. The number of expected topics a model generates in Stage 1 is the denominator for every coverage score in Stage 2 a model that generates 9 topics will always report higher coverage than a model that generates 20, even if the target page has not changed. This makes Stage 1 the single most consequential output of the entire workflow.

Stage 1 topic generation: Claude 20/20, ChatGPT 25/25, Gemini 9/10, Qwen 7/8. Ground truth 17 per vertical.
Figure 3. Expected topics generated per model per vertical, against ground truth of 17. Claude's 20 topics and ChatGPT's 25 are both close to ground truth in scope. Gemini's 9-10 represent significant under-generation through topic bundling multiple distinct topics collapsed into single broad categories. Qwen's 7-8 are generic and non-specific to the query.
3.2
Coverage Score Results

Coverage scores are reported directly from each model's output, then plotted against the manually established ground truth. A model whose Stage 1 topic set is too small will overstate coverage; a model whose Stage 1 is well-calibrated will produce scores close to ground truth.

Coverage scores: V1 ground truth 35%, V2 ground truth 24%. Claude V1 60% V2 25%. ChatGPT V1 64% V2 36%. Gemini V1 78% V2 50%. Qwen V1 0% V2 0%.
Figure 4. Coverage scores per model per vertical vs ground truth (dashed lines). Claude and ChatGPT both overstate coverage on V1 but in plausible range given their more comprehensive topic sets. Gemini substantially overstates both due to a thin Stage 1. Qwen reports 0% on both despite listing topics as both covered and missing simultaneously an internal contradiction producing a meaningless output. Note: all frontier models overstate coverage vs ground truth, suggesting a general tendency to interpret partial coverage as full coverage at the topic level.

SECTION 4
Key Findings

This benchmark produced five findings. Four extend the WP001 findings to this new task type; one is entirely new and unique to the coverage scoring workflow.

F1
Prompt-driven coverage scoring is valid
All three frontier models produced Stage 1 topic sets that were meaningfully aligned with the manually derived ground truth in both verticals. The methodology is not model-dependent it works because the task is well-defined, not because any one model is exceptional at it.
F2
Stage 1 topic count is the critical variable
Coverage score accuracy is determined almost entirely by how many expected topics the model generates in Stage 1. Gemini generated 9 topics and produced a 78% coverage score. Claude generated 20 topics and produced 60%. The target page did not change. Stage 1 quality is the ceiling for everything downstream.
F3
Gemini's failure mode is module-specific
In WP001 (pairwise comparison), Gemini was rated "Usable." In this benchmark it is "Not recommended." The difference is that topic bundling harmless in comparison tasks directly inflates coverage scores in this module. This is not a general Gemini weakness; it is a specific incompatibility with Stage 1 requirements.
F4
Methodology generalises across domains
Claude and ChatGPT produced consistent output quality across both the technical AI/GEO vertical and the practical roofing vertical. Topic counts, entity detection quality, and recommendation specificity were comparable across domains. The module can be applied to any vertical without domain-specific prompt tuning.
F5
Qwen2.5 3B fails for the second consecutive benchmark confirming the 7B+ threshold
The exact same failure mode appeared across both verticals and both benchmarks. Qwen listed Stage 2 topics as simultaneously covered (with checkmarks) and missing (with crosses), then computed 0% coverage despite the contradiction. Entity coverage scored 0% on both verticals. This is the second consecutive benchmark failure with a different task type and different domains confirming that the failure is not task-specific or prompt-fixable. It reflects a fundamental inability to track state across a multi-stage structured output at 3B scale. WP001's finding stands: the minimum viable local model threshold for this class of workflow is 7B parameters or above.
New finding — unique to this module

The most practically significant finding of this research is not which model scored highest. It is that coverage score accuracy is a function of Stage 1 quality, not Stage 2 quality. A poorly calibrated Stage 1 produces a plausible-looking coverage score that systematically misleads users into thinking their page is more complete than it is. This is more dangerous than a clearly wrong output it is a confident, well-formatted, incorrect output. The module must either (1) use a model reliably generating 18-25 expected topics, or (2) enforce a minimum expected topic count in post-processing before calculating the coverage score.

SECTION 5
Implications for AI Spider

The Coverage Score and Entity Expansion module is planned as a future feature addition to AI Spider, sitting alongside the Competitive Content Intelligence module validated in WP001. These findings shape its architecture directly.

  • 01
    Claude Sonnet 4.6 or GPT-4-class as default model
    Gemini 3.5 Flash is downgraded from "Usable" to "Not recommended" for this specific module due to its Stage 1 under-generation behaviour. The module will default to Claude or ChatGPT-class models. Gemini remains acceptable for the CCI module where Stage 1 is not required.
  • 02
    Minimum expected topic guard will be enforced
    A post-processing check will enforce a minimum expected topic count before calculating the coverage score. If Stage 1 produces fewer than 15 topics for a standard query, the system will either retry with a more explicit prompt or flag the result as potentially unreliable. This prevents inflated scores from thin topic sets from reaching the user.
  • 03
    Three-stage output will be surfaced separately in the UI
    Because Stage 1, Stage 2, and Stage 3 are independently useful and because Stage 1 quality is visible and auditable the AI Spider dashboard will surface all three stages as separate panels. Users can inspect the expected topic set before trusting the coverage score, which adds a layer of transparency that pure numeric outputs lack.
  • 04
    Module is domain-agnostic by design
    The roofing vertical result confirms that no domain-specific prompt tuning is required. The module works for any vertical where a user can provide a target URL, 1-4 competitor URLs, and a query. This makes it immediately applicable to AI Spider's primary use cases: local businesses, service providers, and content-heavy marketing pages.
  • 05
    Entity expansion complements RankScan monitoring
    The entity gap output from Stage 3 identifies which named entities competitors mention that the target page omits. When combined with RankScan which tracks how often a brand appears in AI-generated answers entity additions can be prioritised by their observed citation frequency in actual AI responses. This creates a closed loop: identify the entity gap, add the content, then verify the citation rate improves.
SECTION 6
Next Steps

This paper documents the validation phase. The following work is now planned or in progress:

  • 6.1Module build — AI Spider Coverage Score tab: Implementation as a standalone tab independent from the core crawl. Inputs: user query, target URL, up to 4 competitor URLs. Outputs: Stage 1 expected topics, Stage 2 coverage score with covered/missing breakdown, Stage 3 entity gap list with entity coverage score, prioritised recommendations. Post-processing minimum topic guard enforced before score calculation.
  • 6.2Stage 1 minimum topic calibration: Systematic testing of the minimum expected topic count across 10 additional verticals to establish a reliable guard threshold. Current evidence suggests 15 topics is a practical minimum for standard queries; high-complexity verticals (legal, medical, financial) may require 20+.
  • 6.3RankScan integration for entity priority ranking: Matching Stage 3 entity gap output against RankScan citation data to rank missing entities by their observed frequency in AI-generated answers. Entities cited frequently by AI models but absent from the target page become the highest-priority additions bridging the gap between content audit and AI visibility measurement.
  • 6.4Longitudinal validation: Following implementation of coverage-informed content updates on target pages, citation rates will be tracked via RankScan over 90 days to measure whether closing identified gaps produces a measurable improvement in AI citation frequency. This will form the basis of Working Paper No. 003.
  • 6.57B+ local model re-evaluation: Once Llama 3.1 8B or Qwen2.5 7B are tested on the same three-stage prompt, a minimum viable local model recommendation will be published. WP001 established the 7B threshold for pairwise comparison; this benchmark will establish whether a higher threshold applies to the more demanding three-stage task.
Tool Context

AI Spider is a locally-hosted website crawler and AI retrievability audit platform. It performs deterministic crawl analysis across 17 tabs including URL intelligence, AI signals scoring, content similarity, PageRank and HITS authority propagation, N-gram analysis, structured data auditing, and redirect/canonical health. The Coverage Score and Entity Expansion module documented in this paper is a planned future feature addition. RankScan tracks brand mention frequency across AI platforms including ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and Perplexity. MarAI is a multi-client AI-powered marketing content generation platform. Together these tools form an integrated AI-native marketing intelligence infrastructure that this research is designed to extend.

NOTE ON SOURCES

All target and competitor URLs used in the benchmark were publicly accessible pages fetched in April-May 2026. Page content was extracted by the researcher and included verbatim in the analysis prompt. Ground truth expected topic sets (17 topics per vertical) were compiled manually before running any model tests and were not modified after viewing outputs. Coverage score ground truth (~35% for V1, ~24% for V2) was derived from mapping ground truth topics against target page content.

Models tested: Claude Sonnet 4.6 (Anthropic), ChatGPT GPT-5.5 (OpenAI), Gemini 3.5 Flash (Google), Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct (Alibaba, via LM Studio v0.3). All tests conducted April-May 2026.
