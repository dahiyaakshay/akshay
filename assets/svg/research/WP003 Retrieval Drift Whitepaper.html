ABSTRACT

This paper documents pre-build validation research for a Retrieval Drift Analysis module planned as a future feature addition to AI Spider, a proprietary locally-hosted website crawler and AI retrievability audit platform. It is the third paper in a series validating the planned content intelligence layer of AI Spider alongside the Competitive Content Intelligence module (WP001) and the Coverage Score and Entity Expansion module (WP002).

Where WP001 measured what competitors have that a target page lacks, and WP002 measured whether a page covers what a complete answer requires, this module addresses a third and distinct question: does the page stay focused on answering the user's query, or does it drift into topics that dilute its retrieval relevance? A page can be comprehensive and still score poorly on retrieval relevance if a significant proportion of its entity footprint belongs to unrelated domains.

Research involved designing a four-stage prompt (intent extraction, competitor validation, entity classification, drift scoring), building a two-vertical test set using cybersecurity consulting and AI/GEO tools, establishing ground truth drift score ranges manually, and running the identical prompt across three frontier models - Claude Sonnet 4.6, ChatGPT GPT-5.5, and Gemini 3.5 Flash. Qwen2.5 3B was excluded following two consecutive non-viable results in WP001 and WP002.

Research Conclusion

Retrieval drift scoring is a valid and discriminating signal for AI retrievability. All three frontier models produced coherent entity classifications and plausible drift scores. The key finding is a meaningful divergence in how models draw the Aligned/Supporting/Drift boundary: Claude produces the most conservative classifications (fewer Drift entities, lower scores), Gemini the most aggressive (more Drift entities, higher scores), and ChatGPT falls between. This divergence is not a failure - it reflects genuine interpretive ambiguity at the boundary between Supporting and Drift - but it has direct implications for how the module should handle score calibration. The module will use Claude as the recommended default, with a calibration layer to adjust for known model-specific scoring tendencies.

SECTION 1
Background and Research Motivation

Working Papers 001 and 002 established that structured content gap analysis and prompt-driven coverage scoring are valid signals for AI retrievability improvement, and identified frontier models as reliable engines for both tasks. Both modules address completeness - what is missing from a page relative to what it should contain.

The Retrieval Drift Analysis module addresses a complementary but different problem. Completeness asks: is everything that should be here, here? Drift asks: is everything that is here, supposed to be here?

"A page that discusses backlink analysis, domain authority, and SERP features in the context of an AI visibility query is not incomplete - it is mis-focused. Its entity footprint signals to retrieval systems that the page is about general SEO, diluting relevance for users specifically evaluating AI monitoring tools."

This distinction matters in generative AI retrieval. Language models don't retrieve pages based solely on keyword matching. They retrieve pages whose full entity and topic profile most closely matches the intent they have inferred from the user's query. A page that contains the right core entities but also contains a significant proportion of off-intent entities may be outranked by a narrower competitor page - even if the broader page is technically more comprehensive.

This pattern appeared directly in the WP002 benchmark: the Semrush AI Visibility Toolkit page had strong coverage of AI-specific features but also carried the entity footprint of Semrush's broader SEO platform. That entity bleed - backlinks, keyword research, domain authority, SERP features - was identified as a coverage concern in WP002 but could not be measured or scored in that framework. The Retrieval Drift module was designed specifically to surface and quantify it.

Before building the module, three research questions needed answering:

  • Q1Is the Aligned/Supporting/Drift classification reliable? The three-tier entity classification is the core of this module. Do frontier models apply it consistently and in alignment with manually derived ground truth?
  • Q2How much do models disagree on the classification boundary? The Supporting/Drift boundary is inherently subjective. Does model disagreement at this boundary produce materially different drift scores, or do the scores converge despite classification disagreement?
  • Q3Can models reliably estimate competitor drift scores from provided content? The module's competitor comparison feature requires models to estimate drift scores for pages they are only given extracted content for. Is this estimation plausible, or do models fabricate scores?
SECTION 2
Research Methodology
2.1
The Aligned / Supporting / Drift Classification System

The foundational concept of the Retrieval Drift module is a three-tier entity classification system. Every significant entity and topic on a target page is classified into one of three categories, each with a distinct relationship to the original query intent.

Aligned
Directly addresses the user's query intent. An AI model retrieving pages for this query would expect to find these entities. Their presence strengthens citation likelihood.
V1 example: Strategic Advisory, Red Team, Maturity Assessment, Penetration Testing, Regulatory Compliance
Supporting
Related to the domain but not central to answering this specific query. Their presence is acceptable but their weight should not dominate the page's entity profile.
V1 example: Forrester Wave recognition, Pulse Services, SOC integration, Frontline expertise framing
Drift
Moves away from the query's intent. Their presence dilutes the page's retrieval relevance. Not necessarily wrong content for the domain - wrong content for this specific query.
V1 example: Scattered Spider (adversary), Falcon platform product brand, nation-state adversary references

Drift Score calculation

Three scores are derived from the classification. Each measures a different dimension of the page's relevance to the query:

ScoreFormulaWhat it measuresIdeal direction
Intent Alignment ScoreCore intent entities covered / total core intent entitiesHow completely the page addresses what the query demandsHigher is better
Topic Focus Score(Aligned + Supporting) / all page entitiesWhat proportion of the page is on-topic or relatedHigher is better
Retrieval Drift ScoreDrift entities / all page entitiesWhat proportion of the page is off-intentLower is better
Figure 1. The three drift scores and their formulas. Retrieval Drift Score is the primary output - it is the percentage of a page's entity footprint that moves away from the query's intent. A score of 0% would mean every entity on the page directly or supportingly addresses the query. A score above 30% indicates significant dilution risk.
2.2
The Four-Stage Prompt

The module uses a four-stage prompt - one more stage than WP002's three-stage structure. Stage 1 extracts intent from the query, Stage 2 validates that intent against competitor pages, Stage 3 classifies target page entities, and Stage 4 calculates scores. The competitor validation stage is new: it prevents Stage 3 from classifying entities in isolation, anchoring the classification in what the competitive set actually discusses.

STAGE 01
Intent extraction
From the query alone, define core intent. What entities and topics must a page address to answer this query authoritatively?
STAGE 02
Competitor validation
Analyse competitors to extract common entities and topics. This becomes the Competitor Entity Universe - the anchor for Stage 3 classification.
STAGE 03
Entity classification
Classify every significant entity on the target page as Aligned, Supporting, or Drift relative to the query intent established in Stages 1 and 2.
STAGE 04
Drift scoring
Calculate Intent Alignment Score, Topic Focus Score, and Retrieval Drift Score. Estimate competitor drift scores. Generate prioritised recommendations.
Figure 2. The four-stage reasoning framework. The competitor validation stage (Stage 2) is the key structural addition vs WP002. Without it, models classify entities against their own understanding of the query - with it, they classify against what the competitive set actually covers, which is a more grounded and verifiable basis for the Aligned/Supporting/Drift decision.

The full prompt template used across all three models is reproduced below with page content omitted for brevity.

Prompt Template - v1.0 All 3 models identical - Both verticals
Page content omitted

// SYSTEM ROLE

You are a Retrieval Drift Analysis engine. Your task is to measure how focused a TARGET page is on answering a user's PROMPT, and whether it drifts into topics that dilute its relevance to that query.

You will complete FOUR stages of reasoning:
STAGE 1 - INTENT EXTRACTION: From the user PROMPT alone, define the core intent.
STAGE 2 - COMPETITOR VALIDATION: Analyse competitor pages to extract common entities and topics.
STAGE 3 - TARGET PAGE ANALYSIS: Classify every significant entity as ALIGNED, SUPPORTING, or DRIFT.
STAGE 4 - DRIFT SCORING: Calculate scores based on the classification.

Do not use external knowledge in Stages 2, 3, and 4. Stage 1 should reflect your understanding of the query's intent.

// CLASSIFICATION DEFINITIONS

ALIGNED: Directly supports the original prompt intent. An AI model retrieving pages for this query would expect to find these entities.
SUPPORTING: Related to the topic but not central to the query. Acceptable but should not dominate the page's entity profile.
DRIFT: Moves away from the original intent. Their presence dilutes retrieval relevance. Not necessarily wrong for the domain - wrong for this specific query.

// INPUT

## USER PROMPT
[query - e.g. "What is cybersecurity consulting"]

## TARGET PAGE
URL, company name, extracted content

## COMPETITOR 1 through 3 or 4
[extracted content for each competitor - nav, footer, boilerplate removed]

// OUTPUT FORMAT - strictly enforced

STAGE 1 - CORE INTENT
- Core intent entities: [list]
- Core intent topics: [list]

STAGE 2 - COMPETITOR ENTITY UNIVERSE
- Competitor entity universe: [consolidated list from all competitors]

STAGE 3 - TARGET PAGE ENTITY CLASSIFICATION
- ALIGNED entities: [list]
- SUPPORTING entities: [list]
- DRIFT entities: [list]

STAGE 4 - DRIFT SCORES
- Intent Alignment Score: [% of core intent entities the target covers]
- Topic Focus Score: [% of target entities that are Aligned or Supporting]
- Retrieval Drift Score: [% of target entities classified as Drift - lower is better]
- Drift summary: [1-2 sentence plain-language explanation of what is causing drift]

COMPETITOR COMPARISON
- [Target]: [score]% / [Competitor 1]: [score]% / [Competitor 2]: [score]% / [Competitor 3]: [score]%

RECOMMENDATIONS
- Priority 1 (High Impact): [specific actions]
- Priority 2 (Medium Impact): [specific actions]
- Priority 3 (Lower Impact): [specific actions]
Respond only with the structured report. No commentary or explanations outside the format.
Figure 4. Full prompt template used across all three models. Colour coding: green = role constraint and Aligned classification, blue = input blocks and Supporting classification, amber = input structure, purple = output format, red = Drift classification and strict enforcement. The classification definitions block (Stage 3 colour coding) directly mirrors the Aligned/Supporting/Drift spectrum used throughout this paper's visual system.
Key Prompt Design Decisions

Competitor validation before classification: Stage 2 anchors the Stage 3 classification in what the competitive set actually discusses, preventing models from applying idiosyncratic definitions of what counts as Drift for a given domain.

Classification definitions in the prompt: The three-tier system requires explicit definition - without it, models draw the Aligned/Supporting/Drift boundary differently based on their own priors. Embedding the definitions in the prompt reduced boundary disagreement significantly compared to early prototype testing.

Drift cause as a required output: The drift summary (Stage 4) is explicitly required at 1-2 sentences. This forces the model to articulate the mechanism, not just report a number - producing the most actionable output in the entire report.

Competitor scores estimated from content, not hallucinated: Models are explicitly instructed to estimate competitor drift scores based only on the provided content. This was validated in the benchmark - all three models produced plausible estimates that ranked competitors correctly.

2.3
Two-Vertical Test Set

Two verticals were selected to test the module across meaningfully different entity landscapes. The AI/GEO tools vertical from WP001 and WP002 was retained for series continuity. Cybersecurity consulting was introduced as the new vertical - a B2B technical domain with a rich, overlapping entity set that makes the Aligned/Supporting/Drift boundary genuinely ambiguous in places.

  • V1Cybersecurity Consulting - "What is cybersecurity consulting" Target: CrowdStrike Cybersecurity Consulting page. Competitors: Mandiant, Secureworks, WithSecure. Ground truth drift range: 15-25%. Primary drift cause: product platform branding (Falcon) and named adversary references (Scattered Spider) that serve brand positioning rather than query education.
  • V2AI/GEO Tools - "Best AI visibility tools" Target: Semrush AI Visibility Toolkit KB page. Competitors: Peec AI, Rankshift, OtterlyAI. Ground truth drift range: 25-35%. Primary drift cause: traditional SEO entity bleed from the broader Semrush platform - backlinks, domain authority, keyword research, SERP features - that are accurate for Semrush broadly but off-intent for this specific query.

The cybersecurity vertical was deliberately chosen to test an ambiguous case. Entities like AI Security Services, nation-state adversaries, and SOC integration sit at the Supporting/Drift boundary - reasonable people could classify them differently. This ambiguity is the most interesting part of the benchmark: it reveals where models disagree and whether that disagreement is principled or arbitrary.

SECTION 4
Key Findings

This benchmark produced five findings. Three extend the series-level findings from WP001 and WP002; two are unique to the retrieval drift task and have direct implications for module design.

F1
Retrieval drift scoring is a valid signal
All three frontier models correctly identified the primary drift entities in both verticals - the traditional SEO entities on Semrush's page (V2) and the product/adversary-specific entities on CrowdStrike's page (V1). The methodology discriminates meaningfully between focused and unfocused pages.
F2
Core drift consensus - boundary disagreement
All three models agreed completely on the entities that are clearly Drift (backlinks, Scattered Spider, keyword research). All disagreement was concentrated at the Supporting/Drift boundary. This means the most actionable output - the core drift entity list - is reliable regardless of which model is used.
F3
Gemini over-classifies Drift consistently
Gemini classified Supporting entities as Drift in both verticals (Falcon platform, Forrester Wave in V1; 17+ years SEO data, SEO teams in V2), producing above-range drift scores. This is a consistent model-specific pattern, not random error - it is correctable with a known calibration offset. Gemini's aggressive classification is recoverable for this task.
F4
Relative ranking is more reliable than absolute score
All three models ranked competitors in the same order (WithSecure lowest drift, CrowdStrike highest) despite producing materially different absolute scores. For the competitor comparison feature of the module, relative ranking is the operationally useful output. Absolute scores are model-dependent and should be presented with that context.
F5
The drift cause description is the highest-value output - not the score
Claude's drift cause description for V2 (Semrush): "The drift on Semrush's page originates from legacy SEO tooling entities - backlink analysis, keyword research, domain authority, SERP features, and on-page optimisation - that belong to Semrush's broader platform but are irrelevant to an AI visibility query. These entities signal to retrieval systems that this page is about general SEO, diluting its relevance for a user specifically evaluating AI visibility tools." This single paragraph is more actionable than any numeric score. It tells the user exactly what to change and why it affects retrieval relevance. The module's UI should foreground the drift cause description, not the score itself.
Series finding - consistent across WP001, WP002, WP003

Across three consecutive benchmarks covering three different analytical tasks, Claude Sonnet 4.6 has been the most calibrated and recommendation-specific model in every test. ChatGPT GPT-5.5 has been consistently production-ready with slightly less precision. Gemini 3.5 Flash has shown task-specific patterns in each paper: over-inflating coverage scores in WP002 (via thin topic generation), over-classifying entities as Drift in WP003 (via aggressive boundary decisions). These are not general capability failures - they are specific, predictable behaviours that can be accommodated with task-appropriate calibration. The AI Spider module suite should use Claude as the recommended default across all three modules, with documented calibration guidance for users who prefer other models.

SECTION 5
Implications for AI Spider

The Retrieval Drift Analysis module is planned as a future feature addition to AI Spider, complementing the CCI and Coverage Score modules. These findings shape its architecture and UI directly.

  • 01
    Drift cause description is the primary UI element - not the score
    Finding F5 establishes that the plain-language explanation of what is causing drift is more actionable than the numeric score. The module UI will foreground the drift cause paragraph in the results panel, with the score displayed as a secondary metric. Users need to know what to change, not just how bad the number is.
  • 02
    Entity classification chips displayed per entity
    The Aligned/Supporting/Drift classification will be surfaced at the entity level in the UI, not just as aggregate scores. Users will see every significant entity on their page labelled with its classification - making the classification transparent, auditable, and actionable. The colour system (green/blue/red) established in this paper will be used throughout the module interface.
  • 03
    Relative ranking displayed alongside absolute scores
    Finding F4 establishes that competitor ranking is more reliable than absolute score comparison. The competitor comparison panel will display both the estimated drift score and an explicit ranking ("Your page has the highest drift in this competitive set") so users understand the comparative position clearly even if absolute scores differ by model.
  • 04
    Gemini calibration offset documented
    Gemini 3.5 Flash consistently produces above-range drift scores due to aggressive boundary classification. If a user selects Gemini as their API provider, the module will apply a documented calibration note: "Drift scores from Gemini tend to run 5-15 percentage points higher than Claude on comparable pages due to more aggressive Supporting/Drift boundary classification." This preserves transparency without hiding the model-specific behaviour.
  • 05
    Drift analysis integrates with CCI and Coverage Score outputs
    In the AI Spider dashboard, the three content intelligence modules (CCI, Coverage Score, Retrieval Drift) will share entity data. An entity identified as Drift in the Retrieval Drift module that is also absent from the competitor set (via CCI) will be flagged as a double negative - both diluting focus and missing from the competitive entity universe. This cross-module signal will be surfaced as the highest-priority recommendation across all three outputs.
SECTION 6
Next Steps

This paper completes the pre-build validation phase for the Retrieval Drift Analysis module. The following work is now planned:

  • 6.1Module build - AI Spider Retrieval Drift tab: Standalone tab independent from the core crawl. Inputs: user query, target URL, up to 4 competitor URLs, API key. Outputs: entity classification panel (Aligned/Supporting/Drift chips per entity), drift cause description, three scores, competitor comparison ranking, prioritised recommendations. Claude Sonnet 4.6 as default model.
  • 6.2Cross-module entity sharing: Implement shared entity store across CCI, Coverage Score, and Retrieval Drift tabs. Enable cross-module signals - entities that are both Drift (this module) and missing from the competitor set (CCI) to be flagged as highest-priority recommendations across the entire content intelligence layer.
  • 6.3RankScan correlation study: Following drift-informed content updates - removing identified Drift entities and reducing entity bleed - measure whether citation rate in AI-generated answers improves via RankScan over 90 days. This will test whether reducing drift produces a measurable retrieval improvement or whether the effect is masked by other signals. Results will form Working Paper No. 004.
  • 6.4Gemini calibration study: Systematic testing of Gemini's over-classification tendency across 10 additional verticals to establish a reliable calibration offset. If the offset is stable, it can be applied automatically when the user selects Gemini as their model.
  • 6.5Series summary - Working Paper No. 004: A longitudinal paper measuring real-world citation rate changes following module-informed content updates across all three content intelligence dimensions - gap reduction (CCI), completeness improvement (Coverage Score), and drift reduction (Retrieval Drift). This will be the evidence-based capstone of the AI Spider content intelligence research series.
Tool Context

AI Spider is a locally-hosted website crawler and AI retrievability audit platform performing deterministic crawl analysis across 17 tabs. RankScan tracks brand and entity mention frequency across ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and Perplexity. MarAI is a multi-client AI-powered marketing content generation platform. The three content intelligence modules validated in WP001, WP002, and WP003 - Competitive Content Intelligence, Coverage Score and Entity Expansion, and Retrieval Drift Analysis - are planned future feature additions to AI Spider that will sit alongside the existing deterministic audit engine.
