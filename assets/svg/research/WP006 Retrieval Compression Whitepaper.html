ABSTRACT

This paper documents pre-build validation research for a Retrieval Compression module planned as a future feature addition to AI Spider, a proprietary locally-hosted website crawler and AI retrievability audit platform. It is the sixth and deepest paper in a series validating AI Spider's content intelligence layer, following WP001-005.

All previous modules measured structural dimensions of content - what topics are covered (WP002), how focused the page is (WP003), how credible signals appear (WP004), what the topic universe looks like (WP005). None of them measured information quality at the sentence level. Retrieval Compression does: it classifies every significant sentence on a page as a Fact, a Claim, or Evidence, then calculates three ratios - Fact-to-Claim ratio, Evidence Support ratio, and an overall Retrieval Compression Score - that directly predict how much of the page an AI model can extract and cite.

Research involved designing a four-stage prompt with explicit classification definitions, building a two-vertical test set (AI/GEO tools and digital marketing agency) with manually established ground truth counts, and benchmarking Claude Sonnet 4.6, ChatGPT GPT-5.5, and Gemini 3.5 Flash.

Research Conclusion

Retrieval Compression analysis is the most granular content quality signal validated in the AI Spider research series. All three models produced structurally valid outputs and correctly classified both target pages relative to each other. The most important finding is a consistent Fact count inflation by ChatGPT - it classified feature descriptions as Facts rather than Claims, producing 25-30 facts vs Claude's 14-16 and Gemini's 14. Despite this, all three models correctly identified the Semrush KB page as MEDIUM density and the WebFX homepage as HIGH density, and all three competitor rankings were plausible. Claude's classification was the most conservative and most accurate against ground truth. The module will use Claude as default with explicit classification definitions in the prompt to reduce boundary ambiguity.

SECTION 1
Background and Research Motivation

Working Papers 001 through 005 established that AI retrievability is shaped by what topics a page covers, how focused it is, how credible its signals appear, and how complete its entity universe is. These are structural measures - they describe the shape of the content. None of them describe the quality of the individual sentences that make up that content.

"A page can cover every expected topic, have zero drift, high confidence signals, and a complete entity universe - and still be full of unsupported marketing language that an AI model cannot cite, because there is nothing extractable to cite. Retrieval Compression measures what is left when you remove the assertions and look for the facts."

This distinction matters because generative AI models do not cite pages because they cover a topic. They cite pages because they contain specific, verifiable, extractable information - numbers, named events, attributed statistics, quantified outcomes - that can be reproduced in an answer without the model's credibility being challenged. A page that is 80% unsupported claims and 20% facts has low retrieval compression. A page that is 60% facts and 40% evidence-backed claims has high retrieval compression. The score directly predicts how frequently a page is likely to be cited.

This research was motivated by a specific observation from WP004 (Coverage vs Confidence): the Semrush AI Visibility Toolkit page scored HIGH on Coverage and LOW on Confidence, with the Confidence gap traced entirely to the absence of testimonials, case studies, and third-party validation. WP004 identified that the page had some specific numbers but no social proof. WP006 was designed to go deeper: to quantify exactly how many individual facts exist, how many claims exist, what the ratio between them is, and whether the claims are supported by any evidence at sentence level.

  • Q1Classification accuracy: Do models correctly distinguish Facts (verifiable specifics) from Claims (assertions) from Evidence (supporting information)? This is the hardest classification boundary in the six-paper series - feature descriptions in particular sit on the Fact/Claim line.
  • Q2Ratio calibration: Do the Fact-to-Claim and Evidence Support ratios match manually established ground truth? Or do models systematically inflate one category?
  • Q3Score discrimination: Does the Retrieval Compression Score discriminate meaningfully between a low-evidence page (Semrush KB) and a high-evidence page (WebFX)? If both pages receive similar scores, the module is not working.
SECTION 2
Research Methodology
2.1
The Three Content Categories

The module's classification system distinguishes three sentence-level content types. The definitions are precise by design - the most common failure mode in early prototype testing was models treating feature descriptions as Facts rather than Claims, inflating the Fact count and understating the Claim density of marketing-heavy pages.

An objective statement that can be independently verified. Must be specific and concrete.

Critical boundary note: Feature descriptions like "benchmarks brand AI visibility" are Claims, not Facts. They assert a capability without verifying that the capability works as described. A Fact equivalent would be "the Visibility Overview report shows an AI Visibility Score updated daily." The distinction drives the entire Fact-to-Claim ratio calculation.

The Four Compression Metrics

Four metrics are derived from the classification. The Retrieval Compression Score synthesises all four into a single 0-100 signal.

Figure 2. The four compression metrics. Information Density thresholds: HIGH requires Fact-to-Claim ratio above 50% AND at least one evidence item per three claims. MEDIUM requires ratio 30-50% OR limited evidence. LOW is below 30% with minimal evidence.
2.3
The Four-Stage Prompt
STAGE 01
Content classification
Classify every significant sentence as Fact, Claim, or Evidence using explicit definitions.
STAGE 02
Compression metrics
Calculate counts, ratios, and Information Density classification.
STAGE 03
Compression Score
Generate 0-100 score with rationale. Penalise zero evidence regardless of fact count.
STAGE 04
Competitor comparison + recs
Estimate competitor scores. Generate separate recommendations for Facts, Evidence, and Claims.
Figure 3. The four-stage reasoning framework. Stage 1 is the most demanding - it requires reading comprehension at clause level, not topic-level pattern matching. The explicit classification definitions in the prompt directly reduced Fact/Claim boundary disagreement vs prototype testing without definitions.
Prompt Template - v1.0  ·  All 3 models identical Page content omitted

// SYSTEM ROLE

You are a Retrieval Compression Analysis engine. Measure how much extractable, citable information exists on a TARGET page vs COMPETITOR pages.

// CLASSIFICATION DEFINITIONS - explicitly embedded

FACT: Objective statement independently verifiable. Specific numbers, named entities, documented events, measurable limits.
EVIDENCE: Supports a Claim. Named studies, attributed statistics, named case studies with outcomes, expert quotes.
CLAIM: Asserts benefit, outcome, opinion, or conclusion without evidence. Feature descriptions are Claims unless a specific metric is attached.

// OUTPUT FORMAT

STAGE 1: Facts list / Claims list / Evidence list
STAGE 2: Fact Count / Claim Count / Evidence Count / F-to-C Ratio / Evidence Support Ratio / Density
STAGE 3: Compression Score 0-100 + 2-3 sentence rationale
STAGE 4: Competitor scores / Recs split by: Fact density | Evidence | Claim reduction
Respond only with the structured report. No commentary outside the format.
Figure 4. Full prompt template. The classification definitions block is the most consequential structural element - embedding precise definitions directly in the prompt reduced Fact/Claim boundary ambiguity significantly. Without it, all three models treated feature descriptions as Facts, inflating fact counts and understating claim density.

SECTION 4
Key Findings
F1
Retrieval Compression is a valid, discriminating signal
All three models correctly classified Semrush as MEDIUM density and WebFX as HIGH density. Score discrimination ranged from 20 to 32 points between the two pages. The module produces meaningful separation between pages of different information quality - the core validation requirement.
F2
ChatGPT inflates Fact count by treating features as Facts
ChatGPT generated 30 facts for the Semrush page (GT: ~11) by treating every feature description as a separate Fact. "Benchmarks brand AI visibility" is a Claim in the module's taxonomy - it asserts a capability without verifying it works. This is the same over-inclusion pattern observed in WP002-005 but now applied to sentence-level classification.
F3
Gemini under-detects Evidence in high-evidence pages
Gemini identified only 2 evidence items for the WebFX page (GT: ~5-6), missing the 1.8X lead growth benchmark, the 15% ROI improvement, and the manufacturing case study. Despite this, it scored WebFX at 92 - showing that its score calculation over-weighted the high Fact-to-Claim ratio and under-weighted evidence absence. Evidence detection needs calibration for Gemini.
F4
Claude most accurate on both count and score calibration
Claude produced the closest fact counts to ground truth (14 vs GT ~11 for V1, 16 vs GT ~14 for V2), the most accurate evidence detection (8 items for V2), and the only Compression Score within the V2 ground truth range (81 vs GT 78-88). Consistent with its pattern across all six papers.
F5
Competitor rankings are consistent and plausible across all models - Disruptive Advertising correctly identified as lowest
Disruptive Advertising's content - "We've helped hundreds of businesses achieve record-breaking growth," "Our team of over 150 experts crafts data-driven campaigns," "We combine strategic thinking with relentless execution" - scored LOW density across all three models (22-40 Compression Score). This is the correct classification. All three models identified it as the weakest page in V2, consistent with the ground truth expectation. The module's ability to detect pure-claim content is reliable regardless of model.
F6
Recommendations correctly split by type across all models - the most practically useful output confirmed
All three models produced separate recommendation sections for Fact density, Evidence addition, and Claim reduction - not a mixed list. Claude's Fact density recommendation for Semrush: "Replace 'identifies competitive gaps' with a specific description of which data points the gap analysis surfaces." This is actionable at sentence level. Claude's Evidence recommendation: "Add at least one named case study with measurable outcome." Claude's Claim reduction recommendation: "Replace 'generates presentation-ready reports' with factual description of report formats and export limits." These three are genuinely different editorial actions - and this finding validates the module's core premise: splitting the three recommendation types produces more actionable output than a single quality score.
Series finding across WP001-006

Across six consecutive benchmarks covering six different analytical tasks, Claude Sonnet 4.6 has produced the most calibrated output in every paper. In WP006 its Compression Score for WebFX (81) is the only score within the manually established ground truth range (78-88). ChatGPT GPT-5.5 has been consistently production-ready with a systematic over-inclusion tendency (over-counting facts in WP006, over-counting signals in WP004, over-generating entities in WP005). Gemini 3.5 Flash has shown a specific failure pattern in each paper - under-generating topics (WP002), over-classifying drift (WP003), over-estimating confidence (WP004), hallucinating entities (WP005), under-detecting evidence (WP006). All are correctable with calibration. The AI Spider content intelligence suite recommends Claude as the default across all six modules.

SECTION 5
Implications for AI Spider
  • 01
    Classification definitions embedded in UI, not just the prompt
    Finding F2 establishes that even with explicit definitions in the prompt, ChatGPT crosses the Fact/Claim boundary. The module UI will display the three classification definitions prominently in the results panel, enabling users to audit the model's classifications against the stated criteria. Users can manually reclassify borderline items - a feature no other AI Spider module requires because this is the only module where individual sentence classification matters.
  • 02
    Density bar visualisation as primary output
    The content classification produces three counts - Facts, Claims, Evidence - that map directly to a proportional bar visualisation showing the composition of the page's content. This is more informative than a single score: a page with 60% Facts and 0% Evidence looks very different from a page with 40% Facts and 30% Evidence, even if their Compression Scores are similar. The density bar is the WP006 module's primary visual output.
  • 03
    Evidence count calibration for Gemini users
    Finding F3 establishes that Gemini systematically under-detects evidence items in high-evidence pages. If a user selects Gemini as their API provider, the module will display a calibration note on the Evidence count and Evidence Support Ratio, flagging that these figures may be understated and recommending manual review of named case studies and benchmark citations.
  • 04
    Three separate recommendation panels
    Finding F6 confirms that Fact density, Evidence, and Claim reduction recommendations are distinct editorial actions requiring different content work. The module UI will present three separate recommendation panels - matching the WP004 Coverage vs Confidence approach of separating Coverage from Confidence recommendations. A single mixed list would lose the analytical value of having classified at sentence level.
  • 05
    Compression Score feeds the unified content intelligence dashboard
    The Retrieval Compression Score is the sixth metric now available in the AI Spider content intelligence layer, alongside CCI gap scores (WP001), Coverage Score (WP002), Drift Score (WP003), Coverage vs Confidence quadrant (WP004), and Query Universe depth (WP005). A page that is LOW on Compression, HIGH on Drift, and LOW Coverage simultaneously has a complete content remediation profile - and each gap maps to a different editorial action. MarAI, the multi-client AI content generation platform, can generate the specific content types each recommendation calls for: evidence-backed paragraphs, named statistics, and case study summaries.
