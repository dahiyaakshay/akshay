ABSTRACT

This paper documents pre-build validation research for a Multi-Hop Reasoning module planned as a future feature addition to AI Spider, a proprietary locally-hosted website crawler and AI retrievability audit platform. It is the seventh paper in a series validating AI Spider's content intelligence layer, following WP001-006.

WP001-006 measured content as a flat structure - what topics are present, how complete, how focused, how credible, how expanded, how dense. Each module measured the nodes in a page's content graph. Multi-Hop Reasoning measures the edges - the relationships connecting those nodes into logical chains. A page that lists "citation tracking," "brand visibility," and "AI citations" as separate concepts scores differently from a page that explains: citation tracking reveals which sources AI models cite most frequently, optimising for those sources increases citation likelihood, higher citation rates improve brand visibility in AI answers. Both pages contain the same entities. Only one contains a reasoning chain that an AI model can follow and cite.

Research Conclusion

Multi-Hop Reasoning extraction is the most discriminating content quality signal validated in the AI Spider series. All three models correctly identified the Semrush KB page as low-reasoning-depth and WebFX as high-reasoning-depth, but with dramatically different relationship counts: Gemini extracted only 2 relationships from Semrush (scored 15) while ChatGPT extracted 28 (scored 68) and Claude extracted 10 (scored 34). Claude's score fell within the manually established ground truth range (25-40). ChatGPT and Gemini represent opposite failure modes - ChatGPT over-extracted relationships by treating feature-to-tool mappings as reasoning chains; Gemini under-extracted by only accepting the most literal explicit statements. On WebFX, all three models correctly found the 7-hop SEO chain and scored the page in the HIGH range. Claude remains the recommended default for its calibrated extraction style.

SECTION 1
Background and Research Motivation

The seven-paper AI Spider research series has progressively mapped different dimensions of what makes content retrievable by AI systems. Each paper measured a different structural property: topic completeness (WP002), topical focus (WP003), evidence quality (WP004), entity universe completeness (WP005), information density (WP006). Together they describe the shape and quality of a page's content inventory.

"All previous modules measured what a page contains. Multi-Hop Reasoning measures how well the page explains the connections between what it contains. AI models do not just retrieve pages that mention the right topics - they retrieve pages that explain how those topics relate, because a reasoning chain is far more citable than a list of facts."

This distinction is particularly important for AI retrievability because generative AI systems construct answers by following reasoning chains. When a user asks "how does citation tracking improve AI visibility?", an AI model needs a source page that answers not just "citation tracking exists" and "AI visibility exists" but "citation tracking identifies which sources AI models cite most frequently, optimising for those sources increases citation likelihood, higher citation rates improve brand visibility in AI-generated answers." That is a three-hop chain - and it is the type of content AI models extract and cite.

Three research questions needed validation before building:

  • Q1Can models extract explicit relationships without hallucinating? Unlike WP001-006 where models worked from structured outputs, here models must parse prose for causal, enabling, and conditional relationships. The risk of inferring domain knowledge as stated relationships is significant.
  • Q2Do models agree on chain depth? The maximum chain depth is the module's most important metric - it determines whether content supports shallow feature lists or deep reasoning. Do models find the same longest path in both verticals?
  • Q3Does the Reasoning Score discriminate between a low-depth page and a high-depth page? The Semrush KB page is a feature list with almost no explicit reasoning chains. The WebFX homepage explicitly built multi-hop chains into the copy. If models score them similarly, the module is not working.

2.1
The Reasoning Chain Framework

Multi-Hop Reasoning measures content along two axes: connected vs isolated entities, and shallow vs deep relationship chains. The core concept is that every relationship in a page's content can be expressed as a directed triple:

Figure 1. Single-hop vs multi-hop reasoning. The left example describes what citation tracking does. The right example explains the mechanism through which it produces an outcome. AI models can cite the right example as an answer to "how does citation tracking improve brand visibility" - they cannot cite the left example because it does not answer that question.

The Five-Stage Prompt
Prompt Template - v1.0  ·  All 3 models identical Page content omitted

// SYSTEM ROLE

You are a Multi-Hop Reasoning Analysis engine. Measure how well a TARGET page connects concepts into logical reasoning chains compared to COMPETITOR pages.

Only extract relationships EXPLICITLY stated or directly implied in the provided text. Do NOT infer relationships from your domain knowledge.

// 5 STAGES

STAGE 1: Entity identification - Connected vs Isolated
STAGE 2: Relationship extraction - [Source] -> [type] -> [Target]
STAGE 3: Graph metrics - relationships, max depth, connected/isolated counts, coverage %
STAGE 4: Multi-Hop Reasoning Score 0-100 with rationale
STAGE 5: Competitor comparison + relationship gaps + prioritised recommendations
Respond only with the structured report. No commentary outside the format.
Figure 3. Full prompt template. The "do not infer from domain knowledge" instruction is the most critical constraint in the WP007 prompt - without it, models hallucinate reasoning chains that exist in the domain but are not stated in the text. This was the primary failure mode in prototype testing.

SECTION 3
Benchmark Results
3.1
V1 - Semrush: Relationship Extraction Comparison

V1 is the most revealing test in the benchmark because the Semrush KB page has almost no explicit reasoning chains - it is a feature list. The three models produced radically different relationship counts from the same text: 2, 10, and 28. This spread - the largest cross-model divergence in the seven-paper series - reveals the Fact/Relationship boundary problem that is unique to this module.

Relationship counts and scores across models and verticals.
Figure 4. Relationship counts (solid bars, left axis) and Reasoning Scores (outlined bars, right axis) per model per vertical. V1 shows the largest cross-model divergence in the series: Gemini 2 relationships vs ChatGPT 28 relationships from the same page. V2 shows strong convergence - all three models found 18-24 relationships and scored the page in the HIGH range (72-90). The GT range lines show the manually established ground truth ranges.

The divergence traces to a single classification decision: does "Semrush AI Visibility Toolkit enables benchmark brand AI visibility" constitute a reasoning relationship? Claude said no (it is a feature description, not a causal chain - the tool does not "cause" brand AI visibility, it measures it). ChatGPT said yes, and generated 11 such "tool enables feature" relationships. Gemini said only literal causal statements count, and found just 2.

SECTION 4
Key Findings
F1
Multi-Hop Reasoning is the strongest discriminator in the series
The score gap between Semrush (feature list, depth 2-3) and WebFX (reasoning-rich, depth 7) ranged from 22 to 73 points across models. No previous module produced this level of discrimination between the two pages. The module measures a genuinely different content quality dimension that WP001-006 could not capture.
F2
ChatGPT over-extracts by treating feature descriptions as relationships
ChatGPT generated 28 relationships from the Semrush page (GT: ~7) by treating "Toolkit enables benchmark brand visibility" and 10 similar statements as reasoning relationships. These are feature descriptions, not causal chains - the tool does not cause brand visibility, it measures it. This is the same over-inclusion pattern observed across WP002-006, now applied to relationship extraction.
F3
Gemini under-extracts in low-reasoning-depth pages
Gemini extracted only 2 relationships from the Semrush page, scoring it at 15 (GT: 25-40). It accepted only the most literal explicit causal statements ("blocks AI crawlers") while missing "Brand Performance measures Share of Voice" and other clear relationships. This is the most conservative extraction style across the series - useful as a conservative lower bound but not suitable as the sole model for this module.
F4
All three models agreed on chain depth in V2 - the most important metric
Despite V1 relationship count divergence (2, 10, 28), all three models found a maximum chain depth of 6-7 for WebFX and independently identified the same SEO chain as the longest path. Maximum chain depth is more robust to extraction style differences than relationship count - it is the most reliable single metric for cross-model comparison.
F5
Relationship gap identification is the module's most actionable output - agreed across all models
All three models identified the same core relationship gaps for V1 Semrush: the missing chain from "fixing technical issues -> improves AI crawler access -> leads to higher inclusion in AI answers" (OtterlyAI has this chain, Semrush stops at "identifies issues"), and the missing chain from "Share of Voice improvement -> increases brand discoverability in AI answers" (Rankshift has this, Semrush measures SoV but never explains its downstream effect). These are specific, actionable, editorial instructions - not generic advice. The module produces more precise content recommendations than any of WP001-006 because it identifies the specific reasoning chains that are missing, not just the topics.
F6
The Semrush page has high WP002-006 scores but very low WP007 score - a new type of content gap
The Semrush KB page scored reasonably across WP002-006: MEDIUM coverage, MEDIUM density, correct entity universe. But in WP007 it scores 25-34 - the lowest of the seven modules. This reveals a content architecture problem that no previous module could detect: the page lists what features do, but never explains the mechanism by which they produce outcomes. An AI model asked "how does AI Search Site Audit improve AI visibility?" cannot cite this page - it can only find "AI Search Site Audit identifies technical issues blocking AI bots." The chain from identifying issues to fixing them to improved AI visibility is absent. WP007 surfaces this gap for the first time.
Series finding across WP001-007

Across seven consecutive benchmarks covering seven different analytical tasks, Claude Sonnet 4.6 has produced the most calibrated output in every paper. In WP007 its V1 score (34) is the only score within the manually established ground truth range (25-40). ChatGPT GPT-5.5 has been consistently production-ready with a systematic over-inclusion tendency that is now observable across six different dimensions: topics (WP002), signal counts (WP004), entity counts (WP005), facts (WP006), relationships (WP007). Gemini 3.5 Flash has shown a specific task-level failure in each paper. The pattern is stable and predictable. The AI Spider content intelligence suite recommends Claude as the default across all seven modules.

SECTION 5
Implications for AI Spider
  • 01
    Maximum chain depth as the primary displayed metric
    Finding F4 establishes that maximum chain depth is the most robust metric across models - all three agreed on 6-7 hops for WebFX despite different relationship counts. The module UI will lead with maximum chain depth as the primary KPI, with the longest chain displayed as a visual flow diagram. The score is secondary.
  • 02
    Relationship extraction threshold to prevent over-counting
    Finding F2 establishes that ChatGPT treats feature descriptions as relationships, inflating counts. The module will apply a relationship quality filter: only relationships where the source entity causes, enables, improves, or determines an outcome in the target entity are included. Feature descriptions (X measures Y, X monitors Y) are valid as single-hop nodes but not counted as reasoning relationships in the depth calculation.
  • 03
    Relationship gap panel as the primary recommendation output
    Finding F5 establishes that relationship gaps are the most actionable output in the module - more precise than topic gaps (WP002) or entity gaps (WP001) because they specify the exact causal chain that is missing. The module will display competitor chains that the target page lacks, formatted as the explicit chain to add, not just a topic category to cover.
  • 04
    Cross-module integration - WP007 catches what WP001-006 miss
    Finding F6 establishes that a page can score adequately across WP002-006 and still have a fundamental reasoning architecture problem detectable only by WP007. The unified AI Spider recommendations panel will flag a specific alert: "Page has acceptable topic coverage and information density but low reasoning depth - entities are listed but mechanisms are unexplained."
  • 05
    Knowledge graph visualisation in the AI Spider dashboard
    The relationship extraction output (source -> type -> target triples) maps directly to a node-edge graph visualisation. AI Spider will render the extracted knowledge graph as an interactive diagram, showing connected entities as nodes, relationships as labelled edges, and isolated entities as dimmed peripheral nodes. Users can identify at a glance which areas of their content form reasoning chains and which are isolated fact lists.
